This album was, for me, like a small shape visible on the distant horizon that grows as you approach until, actually massive in size, it overtakes your entire field of vision. I had met Colin through mutual friends as he and Mike Lerner were starting Behold the Arctopus. When they became a proper live band, I saw them every chance I could. There was a fire to their live performance that belied the "academic" vibe the band otherwise had. Lev was a high school friend who had been good at drums since a young age; in fact part of the reason I picked up bass was reasoning that if I played with a drummer as good as him and was myself at least mediocre, the band would still seem good overall.

Lev and I were learning how to create death, black, doom and thrash metal through Astomatous and other projects while living in Chicago. Colin joined Dysrhythmia, a formidable band in their own right, but also a prelude to him and Dysrhythmia guitarist Kevin Hufnagel, their collaborative dynanic well-established, joining Gorguts a few years later. Apparently ex-Gorguts guitarist Steeve Hurdle (a genius, RIP) had played Arctopus & Dysrhythmia for founding Gorguts member Luc Lemay, and Luc the vibe of this musical collective had resonated. He wanted to bring their creativity and professionalism into his musical vision.

I felt the same way. I hired Colin to record and play bass & noise on a song for Lev and my doom metal band, Hymn. And when Astomatous finished a multi-year effort to cobble a full length together from our time in Chicago, I hired him to mix & master that as well. In retrospect this album served as our "application" to Krallice-- as a musician, you can hide very little from the engineer who mixes your recordings. All flaws and virtues laid bare.

## Orthrelm

During one of those sessions Colin played something else he had recorded: a powerful and puzzling single-song album called OV by a duo named Orthrelm. It used the pyrotechnic vocabulary of '80s shredder guitar solos and drum fills that were usually accents in heavy rock as the base elements of the composition. It felt informed by modern classical, free jazz, and folk or classical musics from African and Asian areas without coming across as pretentious or scholarly. Later, in 2006, I would see Mick and drummer Josh [name] perform OV in its entirely at the Beat Kitchen in Chicago. I later found out it was the last show of that tour; the physicality of the piece had caused repetitive stress injuries to his arm and they'd had to cancel the remaining dates.

Lev moved back to New York several months before I did. He was casting a wide net for bands and session drumming gigs in the city, and so it wasn't surprising to hear that Colin and Mick had asked him to play on an album they were working on together. Colin had produced Mick's music, and Orthrelm & Behold had done a split release together, but they'd never actually worked on music before. The broad goal as I understood it was to channel a mutual interest in black metal, with particular reference to Ulver's Nattens Madrigal-- notable for its constant three-part polyphony between the two guitars and bass [add footnote]-- and Dead as Dreams by Weakling.

> The album was just supposed to be a one-off; Lev was initially hired as a session drummer.

But as work progressed the group felt like it might have legs as a going concern. Colin and Mick had written and played the bass on the album, but someone would have to play those parts live if there were to be gigs, and I must've seemed the obvious choice because of my history with Lev. I remember meeting Colin at the Alligator Lounge in Williamsburg with Karlynn Holland in late July 2007-- I had moved back to NYC by then-- and he formally asked me to join the band. Karlynn drew the logo on bar napkin, with most of the main features present. She would do the official version a little later on a piece of heavy art paper about the size of a dinner tray.

Perhaps to solidify my inclusion in the band, they asked me to do a guest vocal on one of the songs on that first album. I went to Colin's studio at the time: Menegroth, the Thousand Caves, in Woodhaven, Queens a couple times during the recording & mixing, to do that vocal and for Colin to show me the bass parts for "Forgiveness in Rot," which made use of power chords on the bass and some unconventional harmonies that would be hard to figure out by ear. Later some gigs were scheduled so, with the unreleased album at home, I got to learning the songs.

## Initiated

"Wretched Wisdom," "Timehusk," "Molec Codices," and "Forgiveness in Rot" are Colin-initiated songs, while "Cnestorial" and "Energy Chasms" began with Mick. By "initiated" I mean that the songs were written by that person as full-length compositions on the instrument they play, be it guitar, bass, or synth. The way Krallice songs are mostly written is that a person initiates by writing and recording that single-instrument composition, and then everyone else writes their own part, longitudinally over the entire piece. Through this method I believe the band achieves a radically democratic aesthetic of collaboration, and a higher level of polyphony than what a single composer can usually achieve by breaking their chord changes out into several distinct lines. I've sometimes called this polyphony of thought, or of intention: different lines in the music even concieving of the song in at times different ways. During the writing/demo recording process of the second or third album, I remember Mick commenting that he loved the moment at which the 2nd guitar and bass were added and there were then certain parts where he momentarily didn't recognize his own original song. The initiator can be outvoted, as it were, by the later-contributing instruments.

I've seen the assumption in reviews and interviews that the recordings are constructed out of several separately recorded guitar lines, as if the recording represents four Colins and four Micks each playing a different guitar part, layered over each other all at once. This is false. While there are at times true overdubs of an additional part, the vast majority of Krallice's music is made to be achievable in rehearsal or live. You'd be surprised how dense a composition you can get out of two guitars, bass, and drums when each part is written for maximalism.

Colin wrote and recorded the bass for all songs except "Energy Chasms" and a part or two of "Cnestorial", which were played by Mick. Their bass styles are recognizably different in a way that's hard to articulate; the bass on "Energy Chasms" in particular has a strummy quality I don't associate with Colin. Some of the Colin bass has features that are recognizable from the style of bass playing he does in Dysrhythmia. It might be because I've had to relearn this part for recent gigs, but I think the bassline for "Forgiveness in Rot" deserves special mention for its use of register and balance between strange and conventional harmonic choices. In a live performance, that song can sound pretty different depending on the balance of instruments one has from their position in the room and thus which notes of the overall "chord" between the three instruments prevail.

The first Krallice show was April 10, 2008 at the Luna Lounge in Williamsburg.
